Same person, same salary, same 200 hours delivered. The only thing that changed is which hours you divide the fixed cost by. Spread it over actual hours and every project looks the most profitable it ever will — and the books still reconcile to zero, so nothing trips the alarm. The first job is to find out which approach your system is actually running. Usually it is the one nobody chose.

Three ways to price an hour
①

Standard-Rate, Surfaced

“Surface it”
Denominator

Actual-Rate, Floating

“Dilute it”
Denominator
Actual hours — it floats.
Hours over the day
Overtime buried in a lower rate.
Reads as
Always reconciles to zero. That is the danger.
③

Standard-Rate, Silent

“Ignore it”
Denominator
Standard capacity, locked.
Hours over the day
Overtime never recorded.
Reads as
A legitimate design — for a firm that means it.

Even-handed on the choice; a hard line on choosing Floating by accident. Surfaced and Silent are legitimate designs for different firms. Floating is almost never chosen on purpose — it is the default a practice-management tool reaches for when no one has decided.
